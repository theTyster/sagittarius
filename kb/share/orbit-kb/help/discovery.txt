orbit-kb help discovery: the discovery module (orbit-kb uses discovery)

Start: orbit-kb init DIR --request-file F --uses discovery   (F holds the report word for word)
       With a tracker item: add --source REF, so the request is pinned to the item (orbit-kb help build, PIN).
Keep the models and the logs inside DIR. Record scope, risk, assumption, and not_explored.
Sections: FACTS, ATTESTATIONS, QUERIES, LAPS, then one recipe for each move (SIGNAL and
The hypothesis, SURVEY, SPECTRUM, FLYBY, TRAJECTORY, DISPROVE, GATE, ASKING, HANDOFF), then
REPORT and BACK. Run only the moves that the report needs, in the order that the KB state
calls for. Every move rereads the report. Describe each divergence in a note.

FACTS
  hypothesis(H, Kind, Text)       Kind is defect, feature, or risk
  budget(laps, N)                 the largest lap budget counts
  lap_done(N)                     lap N ended with a human answer
  discovery_evidence(S, From)     From is system or model. A model file or solver log is never system.
  model(M, Tool)                  M is a model file. Tool is smt or tla.
  model_run(Log, M, Digest)       Log is a run of model M at that digest. It counts while M keeps it.
  derived_from(Target, Existing)  the target model changes the existing-world model
  encodes(M, C)                   model M states claim C
  property(M, C, Text)            the property of M that states C (an assertion, an invariant).
                                  orbit-kb report shows it under Formal, beside the claim.
  finding(F, Kind, From)          Kind: root_cause(X) | conflict(Core) | surprise(Case) | trace(T) | scope(Item)
  reason(M, Rule, Text)           why the rule of model M survived its mutation
  raised_by(Q, X)                 finding or hypothesis X raised question Q

ATTESTATIONS
  reproduction  on system evidence: result(H, reproduced) | result(H, not_reproduced) | confirms(F)
  model_result  on a solver log: verdict(M, Kind, Result) | calibration(M, Case, Result)
                | mutation(M, Rule, Result)
                Kind: sanity, bad_case, together, root_cause(R), normal_case(N), preserve(C),
                      applies(C), explore(T)
                Result: sat, unsat, unknown, reached, violated, holds
                Case: example(C) or case(Text); its Result is matches or differs
                mutation Result: killed or survived
  decision      human only: no_go(H, Text) | hold(H, Text). GO is a core approval.
  vote          vote(Round, go|no_go|hold). Each writer votes once in each round.
                Only the largest round counts.

QUERIES
  discovery_ready   clean: a hypothesis exists, and each one that is not superseded has a
                    system result; a claim is current, or such a hypothesis did not
                    reproduce; no current claim is refuted or has no falsifier; no question
                    waits or is to_ask; no WD1, WD3, WD7, ED3-ED7
  discovery_decide  discovery_ready, or budget_spent (the last lap of the largest budget is done)
  no_falsifier(C)   current claim C has no check, and no trusted model gave a preserve(C)
                    or applies(C) result other than unknown
  model_trusted(M)  M is sane, calibrated, and does not diverge; or, one level only,
                    sane, not diverging, and derived_from such a model of the same tool
  artifact_vote(V)  go when discovery_ready and a claim is current; no_go when discovery_ready
                    and no claim is current; else hold
  gate(V), gate_split   the outcome of the vote (see GATE)
  question_waits(Q) a raised question that has no answer
If discovery_ready is false: orbit-kb lint, and orbit-kb whynot 'discovery_ready'.
Ready with no current claim happens only when a hypothesis did not reproduce (WD6).

LAPS
  Each lap touches the current system or a solver, and then the human. A human answer that
  changes a claim supersedes the claim. The new claim needs its own falsifier, so another lap
  follows. Stop when discovery_decide holds. Out of budget is not ready: report what you
  know, each open item, and what you do not know. Only the human raises the budget.

SIGNAL: pin the report
  1. Classify the report: a defect, a feature, a risk (can something happen?), or unclear.
  2. Restate it as one hypothesis, in the scope of the report (see The hypothesis, below).
  3. If you cannot, ask the human. Do not guess.
  4. If the report holds two problems, write two hypotheses. Ask the human if they are one
     ticket or two.
  5. Set a lap budget: three laps by default.
  6. If nothing needs learning, propose the build orbit.
  Record:
  orbit-kb add "hypothesis(h1, defect, 'TEXT')" "budget(laps, 3)"
  The KB counts laps only. Write a time limit in a note.
  To reword a hypothesis, add the new one and supersedes(hNew, hOld):
    orbit-kb add "hypothesis(h2, defect, 'NEW TEXT')" "supersedes(h2, h1)"
  The old one then needs no system result. The new one needs its own.

The hypothesis (the backbone of discovery; the claims, the checks, and the vote depend on it)
  Make its assumptions few and its conclusion strong. List its premises beside it.
  - Assume little. Put no cause, mechanism, data shape, or fix in it. Each one adds meaning
    that the report does not have, and chooses a design before the vote. Do not narrow it to
    the one example that you reproduced. A cause that you find is a finding that explains
    the hypothesis. It is not part of the hypothesis.
  - Make the conclusion strong. Say which outcome the report expects, and which facts decide
    that outcome. Then say that the system can fail to give it.
  - Give it a falsifier: the result that would show it is false. A hypothesis with no
    falsifier has collapsed: it holds for any result, so it says nothing.
  - Remove words. Stop when the next removal makes it collapse or changes its meaning.
  An example of the meaning, not a template. The report: "members got the wrong loyalty points".
  - Too strong: "The tier is read from a cache that loads once." It names a cause.
  - Collapsed: "Points depend on something other than the purchase." No result falsifies it.
  - Weak, with a strong conclusion: "The amount of a purchase and the member's tier on that
    day decide its points. Some purchases get a different number." Falsifier: every
    purchase gets the points that its amount and tier give.
  A weak hypothesis usually takes more work to confirm or falsify, because one example does
  not settle it. Stay inside the lap budget, and list what you did not cover.

SURVEY: touch the current system (discovery never skips it)
  1. Record the world facts that the hypothesis depends on, each with a source.
  2. Reproduce a defect with a test, a script, or a request. For a feature, show that the
     system does not do it now. For a risk, show whether its conditions occur.
  3. If the problem does not reproduce, occurs in a different way, or the feature exists,
     attest a divergence with hypothesis(H), and tell the human what you found.
  Record world facts, each with a source. Reproduce with a test, a script, or a request.
  D=$(orbit-kb subject e1 LOG)         then:  orbit-kb add "discovery_evidence(e1, system)"
  orbit-kb attest reproduction e1 'result(h1, reproduced)'     (or not_reproduced)
  If it does not reproduce: orbit-kb attest divergence e1 'hypothesis(h1)'
  A tagged test can be the subject.

SPECTRUM: claims and tools
  1. Split the hypothesis into claims, as in DECOMPOSE (orbit-kb help core): labels,
     citations of the report text, and dormant questions with defaults. Claims state
     outcomes, not designs. Each claim gets a concrete example and its falsifier.
  2. Choose the cheapest tool that can falsify each claim:
       current behavior                                   a reproduction or a test
       code structure                                     code reading, search, or a KB query
       a quantity                                         a script or a calculation
       rules, permissions, limits, prices, configurations, data constraints   SMT (FLYBY)
       order of steps, retries, timeouts, concurrency, workflows              TLA+ (TRAJECTORY)
       a property for all/every input   universal, for BURN. FLYBY can first search for a
                                        counterexample.
  3. Use a model only when a few examples cannot cover the combinations or orders of steps.
  4. If a claim mixes rules and steps, model the steps in TLA+ with the rules as guards, and
     the rules alone in SMT, with the same claim ids.
  5. Build an existing-world model from the SURVEY facts, and a target model from it and the
     claims. If two tools state one claim, build each statement from the report and the
     SURVEY facts, not from the other statement.
  Record:
  Record the claims (orbit-kb help core) and universal(C) where it applies.
  For a test or script: orbit-kb add "check(t1, c1)" "discovery_evidence(t1, system)"
  then orbit-kb run t1 -- CMD

FLYBY: explore combinations with SMT
  Use Z3 through its Python API or SMT-LIB 2. Give each requirement a name, so an unsat core
  names requirements. Keep the domain small. Use one file for each model, and save each run's
  output as its log.
  1. Sanity. The existing-world rules with no property must be SAT. UNSAT is an encoding
     error. Fix it first.
  2. Calibrate. Pin the inputs of a real case from SURVEY, and of two or three normal cases.
     The model must give the results of the current system. If not, record a calibration
     that differs, or a divergence with model(M), and fix the model. Then the reported bad
     case must be SAT.
  3. Root cause (defects only). Change one suspected rule at a time. The root cause removes
     the bad case and keeps every normal case.
  4. Target. Remove the retired rules, add the introduced rules, and keep the preserved
     rules. Then the bad case and each negated preserved property are UNSAT, each introduced
     condition is SAT, and all requirements together are SAT. That witness is an example for
     the human, and the target's sanity result.
  5. Conflicts. If the requirements cannot hold together, record the unsat core as one
     conflict finding, and ask one question: "For this case, these requirements cannot all
     hold. Which one gives?" After the answer, attest a divergence with claim(C) only on each
     claim that the answer superseded.
  6. Explore. Get several witnesses: block each one, and run again. Show the human the
     surprising cases and the boundary cases: zero, the maximum, empty, one, and many. First
     pin realistic values.
  7. Mutation. Delete each introduced rule, one at a time. The bad case must return (killed).
     If it does not (survived), find out why, and record a reason.
  SAT: a case exists within the encoding. UNSAT: no case exists within the encoding, its
  assumptions, and bounds. unknown: a gap.

TRAJECTORY: explore sequences with TLA+ (the FLYBY steps, with these changes)
  1. Model. Write the variables, the initial state, and one action for each real operation,
     named after its code unit. Include the failure paths that matter: timeouts, retries,
     crashes, and duplicate messages. Record each omitted one as an assumption.
  2. Sanity. For each important state, TLC must violate an invariant that says the state is
     never reached.
  3. Calibrate. TLC must violate the reported good behavior. The code path of its trace must
     exist, and the logs or a test must show the same sequence. If the system shows the
     problem and the model cannot reach it, add the missing action or order of steps.
  4. Target. Add an invariant for each preserved and introduced claim, and liveness where a
     claim needs it, with its fairness assumptions.
  5. Mutation. Remove each new guard, or restore each old action. The violation must return.
  6. Why-not. For each invariant that holds, say why: contradicts (a guard forbids the bad
     state) or absent (the model omits the cause). Record what the model omits as a risk.
  7. Explore. Run TLC on properties that the domain suggests, but that the report did not
     ask about. A violation is a finding. Ask the human if it is in scope.
  8. Bounds. Start with two processes and two or three items. Increase the bounds once, at
     the end. Note the bounds and distinct states of each result.

Solvers: write the models yourself. A subagent on a fast model can read the logs.
Set a timeout on every solver run.

FLYBY (SMT) and TRAJECTORY (TLA+): record
  orbit-kb subject m1 model.smt ; orbit-kb subject log1 run1.log     (a new log for each run)
  orbit-kb add "model(m1, smt)"
  D=$(orbit-kb subject m1 model.smt) ; orbit-kb add "model_run(log1, m1, '$D')"
  orbit-kb attest model_result log1 'verdict(m1, sanity, sat)'
  orbit-kb add "finding(f1, conflict('CORE'), model)" "example(c1, 'witness')"
  For a target model: derived_from(Target, Existing) and encodes(Target, C).
  Record a disproof for each case that breaks a claim.
  TLC results: violated (with a trace), holds within the stated bounds, or not run.
  A violated never-reached invariant is verdict(M, sanity, reached). Use model(M, tla).
  attest model_result reads the log. It refuses a result the log clearly contradicts. Attest what the log shows, or run again into a new log.

DISPROVE: attack the claims
  DISPROVE is shared with the build orbit. In discovery, a fresh subagent sees only the
  report, the claims, and the models. It tries other inputs, orders of steps, larger bounds,
  removed assumptions, old or malformed data, and probes of the current system.
  Record:
  orbit-kb attest disproof c1 "verdict(c1, refuted('price 0 with code X'))"
  Also add the counterexample as an example(...): the build orbit makes it a test case.

GATE: the GO / NO-GO vote (r1 is the request; round 1 first, +1 after each HOLD)
  1. Your vote, sealed, before you give the report:
       orbit-kb attest vote r1 'vote(1, go)'
  2. The human's vote:
       orbit-kb attest vote r1 'vote(1, hold)' --by human
     After that, attest refuses your vote in round 1.
  3. The outcome: orbit-kb query 'gate(V)'. The Vote: section of orbit-kb report shows it.
  4. If orbit-kb query gate_split is true:
       orbit-kb attest divergence h1 'hypothesis(h1)'   and add a note with the three votes.
  5. On GO: orbit-kb subject tk PATH ; orbit-kb add "ticket(tk)" ;
       orbit-kb attest approval tk 'digest(current)' --by human
     On NO-GO: orbit-kb attest decision h1 "no_go(h1, 'REASON')" --by human
     On HOLD:  hold(h1, 'WHAT IT WAITS FOR')
  The seal guards against a mistake, not a look. query and kb.pl show your vote.
  Do not read the human's vote before you vote.

ASKING: ask the human
  Ask as in orbit-kb help core (Asking the human). Also:
  - Ask only the questions that evidence raised: a conflict, a surprise, a trace, a failed
    reproduction, or a scope boundary. Show the concrete case.
  - Ask about conflicts first, then surprises, then boundaries.
  - Questions that no evidence raised stay dormant, with their defaults, in the ticket.
  - When stakeholders conflict, the decision owner decides.
  Record:
  question, assumed, raised_by(Q, X) (X is the finding or hypothesis that raised Q),
  and option(Q, 'Text', 'Effect') for each option, with what choosing it changes.
  Then answer(Q, VALUE, human), and lap_done(N) when the round ends.
  Owners (orbit-kb help build, PIN): a question that waits only on the hub owner is pinned to its
  ledger ask (pin-question Q ledger ASK); any other goes to the tracker (draft questions, then
  pin-question Q tracker ITEM, then pin-answer). When the request changes at its tracker item,
  run pin-request --request-file F.

HANDOFF to the build orbit
  orbit-kb uses build ; orbit-kb lint. The build orbit skips SURVEY.
  The discovery orbit is open until the human approves the ticket after a GO
  (attest approval TICKET 'digest(current)' --by human writes discovery_end). Then readiness
  and the vote are not asked again: a hypothesis that the human restates in build
  supersedes the old one and needs no system result. Only BACK opens the orbit again.
  DECOMPOSE starts from the discovery claims. Each witness, trace, and counterexample
  becomes a test case. The build orbit reuses each red test_result as its baseline.
  A universal claim always burns. A solver or TLC result holds only within its bounds;
  only a Lean proof or a human waiver closes a burn.
  For a claim in SMT or TLA+ and again in Lean, run the SMT witnesses as Lean #eval
  examples. Record a divergence where the two disagree.

REPORT: the discovery report
  Write it from the queries. orbit-kb report prints the build-orbit sections and a Vote:
  section; write the rest yourself.
    Hypothesis:   <one sentence, and its falsifier>
    System:       <reproduced, not reproduced, or exists; the evidence>
    Findings:     <concrete cases, each from the system or a model only>
    Decide:       <up to three questions, with options and defaults>
    Scope:        in: ... / out: ...
    Risk:         <what it touches; what a wrong build can damage; how to undo it>
    Claims:       <each claim, its label, one example, and what would falsify it>
    Not explored: <what discovery did not cover>
    Ask:          "Vote GO, NO-GO, or HOLD." Nothing else: not your vote, and no
                  recommendation. Your vote stays sealed until the human votes.
  On GO, the ticket holds the report without Decide and Ask. It also holds: in the risk, the
  data, money, security, or interfaces at stake, and what the models omit; for each claim, at
  least one example from a witness, a trace, or a reproduction; the human's answers, and the
  dormant questions with their defaults; each counterexample, with its disposition; the
  universal claims, for BURN; the models, verdicts, and bounds.

BACK: back to discovery
  When a divergence shows that the problem is wrong, not the build, file a disclosure, and
  run orbit-kb uses discovery. Record the return:
     orbit-kb attest back h1 "back('The human cannot say what the hypothesis changes to.')"
  It opens the discovery orbit again: a new hypothesis needs its system result (WD7), and
  readiness and the vote are asked again (next round). Then go to SIGNAL if the hypothesis is wrong, SPECTRUM if a
  claim is wrong or missing, FLYBY or TRAJECTORY if a model missed a behavior, and FLYBY and
  then the human if BURN shows that the requirements conflict.
