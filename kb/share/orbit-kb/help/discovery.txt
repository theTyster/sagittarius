orbit-kb help discovery: the discovery module (orbit-kb uses discovery)

Start: orbit-kb init DIR --request-file F --uses discovery   (F holds the report word for word)
       With a tracker item: add --source REF, so the request is pinned to the item (orbit-kb help build, PIN).
Keep the models and the logs inside DIR. Record scope, risk, assumption, and not_explored.
Sections: FACTS, ATTESTATIONS, QUERIES, LAPS, then one recipe for each move (RESTATE and
The goal, SURVEY, SPECTRUM, FLYBY, TRAJECTORY, DISPROVE, GATE, ASKING, HANDOFF), then
REPORT and BACK. Run only the moves that the report needs, in the order that the KB state
calls for. Every move rereads the report. Describe each divergence in a note.

FACTS
  goal(G, Kind, Author, Text)     the goal of the ticket. Kind is defect, feature, risk, or change.
                                  Author is human when the human gave the words, else agent.
  hypothesis(H, G, Author, Text)  a hypothesis under goal G: one avenue to the goal
  defeating_case(H, By, Text)     the case that would show hypothesis H false (it is tempered)
  predicts(H, C)                  claim C is a prediction of hypothesis H (not on a preserve claim)
                                  hypothesis(G, Kind, Text) is the 0.7.2 form of the goal. It still
                                  loads, and a query of it gets a note.
  budget(laps, N)                 the largest lap budget counts
  lap_done(N)                     lap N ended with a human answer
  discovery_evidence(S, From)     From is system or model. A model file or solver log is never system.
  model(M, Tool)                  M is a model file. Tool is smt or tla. (model, model_run,
                                  encodes and model_result are core: a build KB takes them too)
  model_run(Log, M, Digest)       Log is a run of model M at that digest. It counts while M keeps it.
  derived_from(Target, Existing)  the target model changes the existing-world model
  encodes(M, C)                   model M states claim C
  property(M, C, Text)            the property of M that states C (an assertion, an invariant).
                                  orbit-kb report shows it under Formal, beside the claim.
  finding(F, Kind, From)          Kind: root_cause(X) | conflict(Core) | surprise(Case) | trace(T) | scope(Item)
  reason(M, Rule, Text)           why the rule of model M survived its mutation
  raised_by(Q, X)                 finding, goal, or hypothesis X raised question Q

ATTESTATIONS
  reproduction  on system evidence: result(G, reproduced) | result(G, not_reproduced) | confirms(F)
                G is the goal.
  model_result  on a solver log: verdict(M, Kind, Result) | calibration(M, Case, Result)
                | mutation(M, Rule, Result)
                Kind: sanity, bad_case, together, root_cause(R), normal_case(N), preserve(C),
                      applies(C), explore(T)
                Result: sat, unsat, unknown, reached, violated, holds
                Case: fails(Text), a real case where the system fails, or passes(Text),
                      a real case where it passes; its Result is matches or differs.
                      example(C) and case(Text) are the older forms: they calibrate nothing
                mutation Result: killed or survived
  decision      human only: no_go(G, Text) | hold(G, Text) on the goal. GO is a core approval.
  vote          vote(Round, go|no_go|hold). Each writer votes once in each round.
                Only the largest round counts.

QUERIES
  discovery_ready   clean: ready_blocker(X) has no answer. A goal exists, and each one
                    that is not superseded has a system result (a reproduction, or a red run
                    of a system check of an introduce or retire claim, after the goal);
                    a claim is current, or such a goal did not reproduce; each current
                    claim has an example and a falsifier, and is not refuted; no check of a
                    preserve claim fails now; no question waits or is to_ask; no finding
                    of lint WD1, WD3, WD7, WD10, ED3-ED7. While the discovery orbit is
                    closed, it holds
  ready_blocker(X)  each thing that blocks discovery_ready, such as untouched(G), no_example(C),
                    preserve_failing(K). show open and report (Open) name each one
  discovery_decide  discovery_ready, or budget_spent (the last lap of the largest budget is done)
  no_falsifier(C)   current claim C has no check, and no trusted model gave a preserve(C)
                    or applies(C) result other than unknown
  model_trusted(M)  M is sane, calibrated, and does not diverge. Calibrated: a matching
                    fails(...) case and a matching passes(...) case on a current run.
                    A model derived from a calibrated model needs its own two cases
  artifact_vote(V)  go when discovery_ready and a claim is current; no_go when discovery_ready
                    and no claim is current; else hold
  gate(V), gate_split   the outcome of the vote (see GATE). GO cannot win while
                    discovery_ready fails (gate_allowed), and the agent's GO is refused then
  question_waits(Q) a raised question that has no answer
If discovery_ready is false: orbit-kb show open names each blocker, and orbit-kb whynot
'discovery_ready' explains it. The lint codes WD1, WD3, WD7, WD10, ED3-ED7 name some of the
blockers; the others (no goal, a waiting question, a refuted claim, a failing preserve
check) have no lint code. Read the tool, not this list.
Ready with no current claim happens only when a goal did not reproduce (WD6).

LAPS
  Each lap touches the current system or a solver, and then the human. A human answer that
  changes a claim supersedes the claim. The new claim needs its own falsifier, so another lap
  follows. Stop when discovery_decide holds. Out of budget is not ready: report what you
  know, each open item, and what you do not know. Only the human raises the budget.

RESTATE: pin the report
  1. Classify the report: a defect, a feature, a risk (can something happen?), or unclear.
  2. Restate it as one goal, wide: the widest reading that the report allows (see The goal,
     below), with its premises beside it.
  3. If you cannot, ask the human. Do not guess.
  4. If the report holds two problems, write two goals. Ask the human if they are one
     ticket or two.
  5. Under the goal, write each avenue that you will test as a hypothesis, with the case that
     would defeat it. The human goes first: draft your hypothesis and seal it (write it down,
     and do not show it), ask the human for theirs (how they think to get there, and what
     would change their mind), then show both. In a hub the question is an ask to the human
     (orbit hub ledger add ask, with --no-kb or the kb: ref of the ticket). The reproduction
     and the exploration go on while it waits.
  6. Set a lap budget: three laps by default.
  7. If nothing needs learning, propose the build orbit.
  Record:
  orbit-kb add "goal(g1, defect, agent, 'TEXT')" "no_premises(g1)" "budget(laps, 3)"
  orbit-kb add "hypothesis(hy1, g1, agent, 'AVENUE')" "defeating_case(hy1, agent, 'CASE')"
  The author is human when the human gave the words. The KB counts laps only. Write a time
  limit in a note.
  To reword the goal, add the new one and supersedes(gNew, gOld):
    orbit-kb add "goal(g2, defect, agent, 'NEW TEXT')" "no_premises(g2)" "supersedes(g2, g1)"
  The old one then needs no system result. The new one needs its own, or carried(A, g2) for
  the reproduction A of the old one. A hypothesis of g1 counts for g2 only with carried(H, g2).
  To replace a hypothesis with a sharper one under the same goal: supersedes(hy2, hy1).

The goal (the claims, the checks, and the vote rest on it)
  The goal: goal(G, Kind, Author, Text). It is the statement of the intent: one sentence that
  says what the human wants. It names no avenue. Every ticket has one. Discovery exists to
  form it.
  A hypothesis: hypothesis(H, G, Author, Text). It is a claim about one avenue to the goal,
  with the case that would defeat it beside it: defeating_case(H, By, Text). A goal can have
  several. The vocabulary is in orbit-kb help core.
  When the human gives a goal and a first hypothesis, record them in the human's words
  (author human), and sharpen or challenge them at once: name the case that would defeat the
  hypothesis, or say that you find none.
  - Assume little. Put no cause, mechanism, data shape, or fix in the goal. Each one adds
    meaning that the report does not have, and chooses a design before the vote: it is an
    avenue, and an avenue belongs in a hypothesis under the goal. Do not narrow the goal to
    the one example that you reproduced. A cause that you find is a finding. It is not part of
    the goal.
  - Conclude strongly. Say which outcome is wanted, and which facts decide it.
  - Keep it able to fail. A statement that no single case can show false is not tempered: it
    holds for any result, so it says nothing.
  - Put its premises beside it, every time: premise(P, G, 'TEXT'), or no_premises(G). Keep a
    premise only when the report gives it, or when the sentence is false without it. Check
    each premise on the system: premise_checked(P, 'WHERE').
  - Write it wide. Where the report leaves a choice open, write your default into the
    sentence, where the human can see it. For the scope, the default is the widest reading.
  - Remove words. Stop when the next removal empties it or changes its meaning.
  A statement can be wrong in two directions. It is soft when no case can break it: it says
  too little. It is brittle when it breaks on a case that should not matter: it says more than
  anyone gave it.
  An example of the meaning, not a template. The report: "members got the wrong loyalty points".
  - Brittle: "The tier is read from a cache that loads once." It names a cause.
  - Soft: "Points depend on something other than the purchase." No case shows it false: it is
    not tempered.
  - Fit: "The amount of a purchase and the member's tier on that day decide its points."
    Premise: the points table did not change in that time.
  The report says one thing more: "some purchases get a different number today". That is a
  finding about the system, and the reproduction tests it. Keep it apart from the goal
  (finding(F, surprise('CASE'), system)). After the build the finding is false, and the
  goal still stands. An avenue, such as "read the tier on the day of the purchase", is a
  hypothesis under the goal, with its defeating case beside it.
  There are two levels of judgment, and each has its own witness.
  - The system is the witness of one: a result that the system shows, such as a purchase
    whose points differ from what its amount and tier give. Before the build, that result is
    the reproduction. After the build, it is a verdict on a claim: meets, conflicts, or
    unresolved. A conflict is located (the build, the measurement, a premise, or the
    hypothesis) before anything is reopened.
  - The human is the witness of the other: a concrete case that the human judges. Only the
    human judges the goal. Show a case just inside or just outside the edge of the goal or
    of a hypothesis, and ask: "is this case what you want?"
  A wide goal usually takes more work to confirm or falsify, because one example does
  not settle it. Stay inside the lap budget, and list what you did not cover.

SURVEY: touch the current system (discovery never skips it)
  1. Record the world facts that the goal depends on, each with a source.
  2. Reproduce a defect with a test, a script, or a request. For a feature, show that the
     system does not do it now. For a risk, show whether its conditions occur.
  3. If the problem does not reproduce, occurs in a different way, or the feature exists,
     attest a divergence with hypothesis(G) (it names the goal), and tell the human what you
     found.
  Record world facts, each with a source. Reproduce with a test, a script, or a request.
  D=$(orbit-kb subject e1 LOG)         then:  orbit-kb add "discovery_evidence(e1, system)"
  orbit-kb attest reproduction e1 'result(g1, reproduced)'     (or not_reproduced)
  If it does not reproduce: orbit-kb attest divergence e1 'hypothesis(g1)'
  A tagged test can be the subject. A red run of a check tagged discovery_evidence(K, system)
  of an introduce or a retire claim is the system result of each current goal that was
  recorded before the run: no reproduction attestation is needed on top. A red run of the
  check of a preserve claim is not: what must stay true is false now, and it blocks readiness.

SPECTRUM: claims and tools
  1. Split the goal into claims, as in DECOMPOSE (orbit-kb help core): labels,
     citations of the report text, and dormant questions with defaults. Claims state
     outcomes, not designs. Each claim gets a concrete example and its falsifier. A claim
     that only one avenue makes is a prediction of its hypothesis: predicts(hy1, c1).
  2. Choose the cheapest tool that can falsify each claim:
       current behavior                                   a reproduction or a test
       code structure                                     code reading, search, or a KB query
       a quantity                                         a script or a calculation
       rules, permissions, limits, prices, configurations, data constraints   SMT (FLYBY)
       order of steps, retries, timeouts, concurrency, workflows              TLA+ (TRAJECTORY)
       a property for all/every input   universal, for BURN. FLYBY can first search for a
                                        counterexample.
  3. Use a model only when a few examples cannot cover the combinations or orders of steps.
  4. One formal language at a time: one formal language first, a second one only when a
     question is left. If a claim mixes rules and steps, choose the language for the part that
     is in doubt (TLA+ for the order of steps, SMT for the rules), finish it, and record its
     result. Use the other language only when that result leaves a question open.
  5. Build an existing-world model from the SURVEY facts, and a target model from it and the
     claims. If two tools state one claim, build each statement from the report and the
     SURVEY facts, not from the other statement.
  Record:
  Record the claims (orbit-kb help core) and universal(C) where it applies.
  For a test or script: orbit-kb add "check(t1, c1)" "checks_that(t1, 'what it checks, in plain words')" "discovery_evidence(t1, system)"
  then orbit-kb run t1 -- CMD

FLYBY: explore combinations with SMT
  Use Z3 through its Python API or SMT-LIB 2. Give each requirement a name, so an unsat core
  names requirements. Keep the domain small. Use one file for each model, and save each run's
  output as its log.
  1. Sanity. The existing-world rules with no property must be SAT. UNSAT is an encoding
     error. Fix it first.
  2. Calibrate. Pin the inputs of a real case from SURVEY, and of two or three normal cases.
     The model must give the results of the current system. Record a real case where the
     system fails and a real case where it passes; both must match:
       orbit-kb attest model_result log1 "calibration(m1, fails('CASE'), matches)"
       orbit-kb attest model_result log1 "calibration(m1, passes('CASE'), matches)"
     One case does not calibrate a model, and a derived (target) model needs its own two.
     If not, record a calibration that differs, or a divergence with model(M), and fix the
     model. Then the reported bad case must be SAT.
  3. Root cause (defects only). Change one suspected rule at a time. The root cause removes
     the bad case and keeps every normal case.
  4. Target. Remove the retired rules, add the introduced rules, and keep the preserved
     rules. Then the bad case and each negated preserved property are UNSAT, each introduced
     condition is SAT, and all requirements together are SAT. That witness is an example for
     the human, and the target's sanity result.
  5. Conflicts. If the requirements cannot hold together, record the unsat core as one
     conflict finding, and ask one question: "For this case, these requirements cannot all
     hold. Which one gives?" After the answer, attest a divergence with claim(C) only on each
     claim that the answer superseded.
  6. Explore. Get several witnesses: block each one, and run again. Show the human the
     surprising cases and the boundary cases: zero, the maximum, empty, one, and many. First
     pin realistic values.
  7. Mutation. Delete each introduced rule, one at a time. The bad case must return (killed).
     If it does not (survived), find out why, and record a reason.
  SAT: a case exists within the encoding. UNSAT: no case exists within the encoding, its
  assumptions, and bounds. unknown: a gap.

TRAJECTORY: explore sequences with TLA+ (the FLYBY steps, with these changes)
  1. Model. Write the variables, the initial state, and one action for each real operation,
     named after its code unit. Include the failure paths that matter: timeouts, retries,
     crashes, and duplicate messages. Record each omitted one as an assumption.
  2. Sanity. For each important state, TLC must violate an invariant that says the state is
     never reached.
  3. Calibrate. TLC must violate the reported good behavior. The code path of its trace must
     exist, and the logs or a test must show the same sequence. If the system shows the
     problem and the model cannot reach it, add the missing action or order of steps.
  4. Target. Add an invariant for each preserved and introduced claim, and liveness where a
     claim needs it, with its fairness assumptions.
  5. Mutation. Remove each new guard, or restore each old action. The violation must return.
  6. Why-not. For each invariant that holds, say why: contradicts (a guard forbids the bad
     state) or absent (the model omits the cause). Record what the model omits as a risk.
  7. Explore. Run TLC on properties that the domain suggests, but that the report did not
     ask about. A violation is a finding. Ask the human if it is in scope.
  8. Bounds. Start with two processes and two or three items. Increase the bounds once, at
     the end. Note the bounds and distinct states of each result.

Solvers: write the models yourself. A subagent on a fast model can read the logs.
Set a timeout on every solver run.

FLYBY (SMT) and TRAJECTORY (TLA+): record
  orbit-kb subject m1 model.smt ; orbit-kb add "model(m1, smt)"
  orbit-kb model-run m1     (z3, else cvc5; for a tla model tlc, with --spec, --cfg, --with FILE: orbit help model-run)
      It runs the tool itself, keeps runs/N-m1.log as the subject m1_runN and writes model_run. A model_run typed by hand is refused.
      Exit 2: unknown, timeout, a failing tool: the log is kept, no model_run is written, it counts for no claim.
  orbit-kb attest model_result m1_run1 'verdict(m1, sanity, sat)'
  orbit-kb add "finding(f1, conflict('CORE'), model)" "example(c1, 'witness')"
  For a target model: derived_from(Target, Existing) and encodes(Target, C).
  Record a disproof for each case that breaks a claim.
  TLC results: violated (with a trace), holds within the stated bounds, or not run.
  A violated never-reached invariant is verdict(M, sanity, reached). Use model(M, tla).
  attest model_result reads the log. It refuses a result the log clearly contradicts. Attest what the log shows, or run again into a new log.

DISPROVE: attack the claims
  DISPROVE is shared with the build orbit. In discovery, a fresh subagent sees only the
  report, the claims, and the models. It tries other inputs, orders of steps, larger bounds,
  removed assumptions, old or malformed data, and probes of the current system.
  Record:
  orbit-kb attest disproof c1 "verdict(c1, refuted('price 0 with code X'))"
  Also add the counterexample as an example(...): the build orbit makes it a test case.

GATE: the GO / NO-GO vote (r1 is the request; round 1 first, +1 after each HOLD)
  1. Your vote, sealed, before you give the report:
       orbit-kb attest vote r1 'vote(1, go)'
     While discovery is not ready, attest refuses your GO and names what blocks readiness
     (orbit-kb show open names it too). Vote no_go or hold, or clear the blockers first.
     GO cannot win the gate while discovery is not ready, whenever the votes came. The
     human's vote is never refused.
  2. The human's vote:
       orbit-kb attest vote r1 'vote(1, hold)' --by human
     After that, attest refuses your vote in round 1.
  3. The outcome: orbit-kb query 'gate(V)'. The Vote: section of orbit-kb report shows it.
  4. If orbit-kb query gate_split is true:
       orbit-kb attest divergence g1 'hypothesis(g1)'   and add a note with the three votes.
  5. On GO: orbit-kb subject tk PATH ; orbit-kb add "ticket(tk)" ;
       orbit-kb attest approval tk 'digest(current)' --by human
     On NO-GO: orbit-kb attest decision g1 "no_go(g1, 'REASON')" --by human
     On HOLD:  hold(g1, 'WHAT IT WAITS FOR')
  The seal guards against a mistake, not a look. query and kb.pl show your vote.
  Do not read the human's vote before you vote.

ASKING: ask the human
  Ask as in orbit-kb help core (Asking the human). Also:
  - Ask only the questions that evidence raised: a conflict, a surprise, a trace, a failed
    reproduction, or a scope boundary. Show the concrete case.
  - Ask about conflicts first, then surprises, then boundaries.
  - Questions that no evidence raised stay dormant, with their defaults, in the ticket.
  - When stakeholders conflict, the decision owner decides.
  Record:
  question, assumed, raised_by(Q, X) (X is the finding, goal, or hypothesis that raised Q),
  and option(Q, 'Text', 'Effect') for each option, with what choosing it changes.
  Then answer(Q, VALUE, human), and lap_done(N) when the round ends.
  Owners (orbit-kb help build, PIN): a question that waits only on the hub owner is pinned to its
  ledger ask (pin-question Q ledger ASK); any other goes to the tracker (draft questions, then
  pin-question Q tracker ITEM, then pin-answer). When the request changes at its tracker item,
  run pin-request --request-file F.

HANDOFF to the build orbit
  orbit-kb uses build ; orbit-kb lint. The build orbit skips SURVEY.
  The discovery orbit is open until the human approves the ticket after a GO
  (attest approval TICKET 'digest(current)' --by human writes discovery_end, when gate(go)
  and the artifacts' go hold in the current round, and the approval is not stale). Then
  readiness and the vote are not asked again: a goal that the human corrects in build
  (goal g2 in the human's words, supersedes(g2, g1)) needs no system result. Only BACK
  opens the orbit again.
  After that, the agent's vote is refused, and a vote of the human does not count.
  A KB whose ticket the human approved before 0.3.0 has no discovery_end, so its orbit is
  still open: a second approval of the ticket, after a GO in the current round, closes it.
  DECOMPOSE starts from the discovery claims. Each witness, trace, and counterexample
  becomes a test case. The build orbit reuses each red test_result as its baseline.
  A universal claim always burns: only a Lean proof or a human waiver closes it. A claim
  that was contested twice owes a formal statement: a Lean proof, or a decided result of a
  trusted model that encodes it, closes it (record the bounds: run_bounds(LOG, TEXT)).
  A solver or TLC result holds only within its bounds.
  One formal language first: a second language only when a question is left (a universal
  burn needs Lean; the solver result does not close it). For a claim in SMT or TLA+ and again
  in Lean, run the SMT witnesses as Lean #eval examples. Record a divergence where the two disagree.

REPORT: the discovery report
  orbit-kb report drafts it from the facts: Hypothesis (the goal with its premises, and each
  hypothesis under it), System (what the system showed), Findings (each from the system or a
  model only), Claims (each with its example and its checks, the falsifiers), Formal, Scope,
  Risk, Not explored, and a Vote:
  section that keeps your vote sealed. Write Decide and Ask yourself.
    Hypothesis:   <the goal in one sentence; each hypothesis with its defeating case>
    System:       <reproduced, not reproduced, or exists; the evidence>
    Findings:     <concrete cases, each from the system or a model only>
    Decide:       <up to three questions, with options and defaults>
    Scope:        in: ... / out: ...
    Risk:         <what it touches; what a wrong build can damage; how to undo it>
    Claims:       <each claim, its label, one example, and what would falsify it>
    Not explored: <what discovery did not cover>
    Ask:          "Vote GO, NO-GO, or HOLD." Nothing else: not your vote, and no
                  recommendation. Your vote stays sealed until the human votes.
  On GO, the ticket holds the report without Decide and Ask. It also holds: in the risk, the
  data, money, security, or interfaces at stake, and what the models omit; for each claim, at
  least one example from a witness, a trace, or a reproduction; the human's answers, and the
  dormant questions with their defaults; each counterexample, with its disposition; the
  universal claims, for BURN; the models, verdicts, and bounds.

BACK: back to discovery
  A hypothesis that a located conflict or a judged case defeats comes back to discovery.
  Re-evaluate the goal, the premises, and the measurement, and temper a new hypothesis under
  the goal. The case stays on the old hypothesis.
  A goal comes back only when you can no longer say what it changes to. When the human says
  what it changes to, build records the new goal in the human's words (supersedes; orbit-kb
  help build), and stays in build. Else file a disclosure, run orbit-kb uses discovery, and
  record the return:
     orbit-kb attest back g1 "back('The human cannot say what the goal changes to.')"
  It opens the discovery orbit again: a new goal needs its system result (WD7), and
  readiness and the vote are asked again: the votes before the back do not count, so both
  vote again (next round) before an approval can close the orbit. A back names the goal,
  and only a closed orbit has a back (attest refuses it otherwise). Then go to RESTATE if the hypothesis is wrong or the goal is, SPECTRUM if a
  claim is wrong or missing, FLYBY or TRAJECTORY if a model missed a behavior, and FLYBY and
  then the human if BURN shows that the requirements conflict.
