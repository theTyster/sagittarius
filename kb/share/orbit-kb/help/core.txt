orbit-kb help core: facts, attestation types, and queries that every KB has

Write facts with: orbit-kb add "claim(c1, introduce, 'Primary buttons are purple.')"
Quote text with single marks. Do not edit kb.pl by hand.

Vocabulary (generated; docs/vocabulary.py)
  A goal is what the human hopes to accomplish: the intent. It names no avenue.
  A hypothesis is a claim about an avenue to the goal: how we think we will get there.
  A premise is a condition that a goal or a hypothesis takes as given. Where it does not hold,
    the statement says nothing.
  A claim is a prediction: what must be observable if its hypothesis and the premises hold.
  The specification is the procedure of the experiment: the claims, and the check of each claim.
  An experiment is one test of one claim: a procedure, its measurement premises, an observation,
    and one verdict.
  A verdict is the result of one experiment: meets, conflicts, or unresolved.
  A hypothesis is tempered when one admissible case can demonstrate that its claim is false.
End of vocabulary

Facts
  request(Id, Text)              the human's words, word for word. A decision that the human gives
                                 during the ticket goes in as a further request, in the human's own
                                 words: add "request(r2, 'only on the settings page')" --by human.
                                 report prints each request under KB.
  goal(G, Kind, Author, Text)    the goal of the ticket: what the human hopes to accomplish. It names
                                 no avenue. Author is human, agent, human:NAME or agent:NAME.
  hypothesis(H, Goal, Author, Text)   a hypothesis under goal Goal: a claim about an avenue to the
                                 goal. A goal can have several. A ticket with a goal and no
                                 hypothesis is valid: its claims hang on the goal.
  supports(G, Ref)               the goal above goal G: a goal id, or project(PG) for a goal of the
                                 project KB. lint W14 names a goal with none (new KBs only).
  defeating_case(H, By, Text)    the admissible case that would show hypothesis H false. With one,
                                 H is tempered. lint W13 names a current hypothesis with none.
  untempered(H, By, Text)        the human or the agent marks H as not tempered, and why
  predicts(H, Claim)             the claim is a prediction of hypothesis H. A claim with no predicts
                                 fact is a claim of the goal. A preserve claim is always one (E21).
  defeat(D, H, Text)             case D defeated hypothesis H. The case stays on H.
  adoption_cited(H, Words)       your reading: the human adopted H, in these words. Alone it adopts
                                 nothing: a human adoption, or a later GO vote of the human, does.
  carried(X, G)                  record X of an older goal is carried over to goal G
  hypothesis(Id, Kind, Text)     the 0.7.2 form of the goal. It still loads, and gives goal(Id, Kind, agent, Text).
                                 Kind is defect, feature, risk, or change (a direct build of a
                                 document or a configuration).
  premise(P, Hyp, Text)          a condition that the hypothesis takes as given
  premise_checked(P, Where)      you checked premise P on the system, at Where (file:line, a query)
  no_premises(Hyp)               "Premises: none". lint W10 names a hypothesis with neither,
                                 W11 one with both.
                                 A premise that the system shows false: record a divergence on
                                 the hypothesis, and restate it (a new hypothesis supersedes it).
  claim(Id, Label, Text)         Label is preserve (true now, must stay true),
                                 retire (true now, must become false),
                                 or introduce (false now, must become true)
  cites(Claim, Text)             the request text that the claim serves
  example(Claim, Text)           a concrete case of the claim
  supersedes(New, Old)           New replaces Old. Never edit a claim or a hypothesis.
                                 Both are claims, or both are hypotheses.
                                 Nothing can replace itself, also through a chain.
                                 add refuses it, and lint reports E15.
  signal(Id, Kind, NoticedBy, Text)   a signal that fired, and what differed (Text).
                                 Kind: questions, divergence, built_by_other, burn, handoff, or effort
                                 (the six signals of the prompt). NoticedBy: agent, system1, or human.
                                 report drafts Signals from these facts, the divergence attestations,
                                 and the burns: "no" for a kind with no record.
  lean_back(Id, Text)            a lean back: "Leaning back: <what stopped>. Still active: <what
                                 continues>." report prints it under Signals.
  proposed(P, system1, FactText, Time)   a proposal of System 1: a fact as text. It is not a record and
                                 it blocks nothing. orbit-kb propose --from system1 'FACT' [--note TEXT]
                                 writes it; orbit-kb show proposals lists each one.
  proposal_confirmed(P, By, Time)   the agent or the human confirmed proposal P: orbit-kb
                                 confirm-proposal P [--by human] adds its fact as add does, with the
                                 usual checks, and writes this fact.
  universal(Claim)               the claim is about every input; it burns (orbit-kb help build)
  check(K, Claim)                K is an executable check of the claim. A check never moves:
                                 for a claim that replaces it, add a new check (lint W5).
  checks_that(K, Text)           what check K checks, in words that a human reads. The Desk shows
                                 them (else the docstring of the check script, else the command).
                                 A new check(K, C) needs it in the same add (E20: add refuses it otherwise).
                                 Lint W12 names an old check of a current claim with none.
  world(Fact), source(Fact, Where)   the current state, and where you saw it.
                                 A fact that you did not record is false.
  question(Q, Claim, Text), assumed(Q, Default), answer(Q, Answer, By)
                                 A question can hang on a hypothesis: its default marks a default
                                 of the hypothesis ("every screen"). report lists it under Guesses.
  option(Q, Text, Effect)        an option of question Q, and what choosing it changes. The question
                                 view (orbit-kb help view) shows each option with its effect. It changes no rule.
  due(X, 'TIME')                 claim, check, or question X is due at TIME. While X is open
                                 after TIME, blocking(past_due(X)) holds.
  ticket(S)                      subject S holds the ratified ticket
  scope(in|out, Text), risk(Id, Text), assumption(Id, Text), not_explored(Text)
  note(X, Text)                  a note about an existing id X (a claim, a question, r1).
                                 It does not create an id.

Artifacts and records
  orbit-kb subject s1 PATH       record an artifact and compute its digest
  orbit-kb attest TYPE SUBJECT 'PREDICATE' [--by human|agent]
  Never type a digest. Put it through the shell:
    D=$(orbit-kb subject M PATH)   then use '$D' in the fact.
  The predicate digest(current) means the recorded digest of the subject.

Who made a record (D28)
  actor(A, Role, Session)        orbit-kb writes it after each attestation (attest, run, prove,
                                 add) and each change. Role is --as ROLE of the command, else
                                 ORBIT_KB_ROLE (build, review, discovery, a subagent label),
                                 else unknown. Session is the writer of the KB: the first of
                                 ORBIT_KB_WRITER, CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID, CLAUDE_SESSION_ID,
                                 HUB_SESSION, else unknown. --by human|agent keeps its meaning.
                                 For run, put --as before --: orbit-kb run --as review k1 -- CMD
  review_saw(R, Kind, Digest)    what reviewer R saw: the ticket, the KB, and the diff.
                                 orbit-kb review start --as R --ticket F --diff A..B writes it.
  rests_on_run(V, Run)           verdict V rests on run Run (attest disproof --rests-on).
  add refuses these three facts: orbit-kb writes them. A limit: the role is self-reported.
  orbit-kb cannot check it, as it cannot check --by human.

Migrations (kb-migrations; orbit-kb help migrate). Only orbit-kb migrate apply writes these:
  retired(N/A, Version, What)    predicate N/A was retired in that orbit-kb version; What replaces it.
                                 Beside it, above the first fact of N/A, the cut-fail clause
                                 N(_, ..., _) :- !, fail. takes the facts out of the model: they stay
                                 in kb.pl, and no rule and no query finds them (lint WM1). It also
                                 declares N/A, so a query of it has no answer, not an error.
  migration_added(Mig, Version, Fact)   migration Mig, run by that orbit-kb version, added Fact.
  migration_question(Q)          (rule) a migration raised question Q for a human step. to_ask(Q)
                                 holds until a human answers it, so done and discovery_ready wait.
  The tools catch mistakes, not cheating.

Attestation types
  disclosure   guess(Q) | unverified(Text) | disagreement(Text) | out_of_scope(Text)
               | self_error(Text) | other(Text)                      agent or human
  divergence   claim(C) | hypothesis(H) | model(M)                   human or agent
               Put the description in a note.
  disproof     verdict(C, upheld(Text) | refuted(Text) | inconclusive(Text) | abstained(Text))
               agent. The text says what was tried. upheld(Text): the attempt did not break
               the claim. Put the counterexample in the text of refuted. The bare inconclusive
               and abstained are still valid (old KBs). Name the runs that the verdict rests
               on: attest disproof C 'verdict(C, upheld(...))' --rests-on a7,a9 writes
               rests_on_run(V, a7) and rests_on_run(V, a9). Each must be a test_result.
  approval     digest(current)                                       human only
  test_result  written by orbit-kb run only

Queries (orbit-kb query 'current(C)' -n 0)
  current(C)         claim C is not superseded
  defeated(Q)        a human answer differs from the default of Q
  to_ask(Q)          Q is unanswered, no human waived it, and its claim is current and contested
  contested(C)       one or more divergences name C
  contested_twice(C) two or more divergences name C
  refuted(C)         a verdict says that C is refuted
  passed(K)          the latest run of K passed
  was_red(K)         K failed before it passed
  stale(A)           approval A is for a digest that is no longer current
  premise_unchecked(P)  nobody checked premise P on the system: a guess (report, Guesses)
  blocking(X)        X blocks done: to_ask(Q), burn_open(C), flare_open(C), under(C), over(X),
                     check_failing(K), check_unrun(K), waiver_lapsed(W), past_due(X),
                     supersede_cycle(C), no_claim. orbit-kb show blocking prints each with its text.

Local rules go in rules.pl beside kb.pl. The first clause of a head declares it. Describe it in a
line above that clause: %% doc: TEXT (lint W9 names a head with none). They cannot change done, or the burn and flare
rules (lint reports E16). not p means that p has no answer (negation as failure), so
not under(_) means "no under at all". A predicate must not depend on itself through not
(lint E18). There is no classical negation: a fact that is not recorded is false.
If the schema does not fit, add a fact of a new predicate. Its first use declares it
(orbit-kb help add), and lint lists it (W7) for review, and flags a name that looks like a
typo of a declared one (E19). The guard has a cost: a real name 2 edits from a declared
one, such as cited/2 next to cites/2, is refused too. Then choose another name. In the
first add of a new kind, add may take one line of description:
orbit-kb add 'FACT' --doc TEXT. It is optional. A kind of kb.pl with no description can
get one later, in an add of one of its facts with --doc (once; a description is never
rewritten). orbit-kb show kinds lists each kind with its description, and lint W9 names a
kind with none.

Rules for the KB
  Size the KB to the work. A small change needs a few facts: the request, one claim, and one
  check. Write a fact only if a query or the delivery report reads it.
  Put the KB where it is useful. Commands find it from --dir, then $ORBIT_KB, then the nearest
  parent directory that holds one. Do not commit the KB or its runs/ logs unless the human
  asks: the delivery carries the evidence. In a hub (a project with HUB-CORE.md) the spec KB
  lives at specs/<slug>/ and is committed, because git is the archive.
  When the work moves to the other orbit, add its module (orbit-kb uses build, or orbit-kb
  uses discovery), then run orbit-kb lint. The new module can add findings.

DECOMPOSE: claims and questions
  Every move reads the ticket text itself, so that an error in an earlier move does not pass on.
  1. Split the ticket into claims. Give each claim one label (preserve, retire, introduce).
  2. Each claim cites the ticket text that it serves. A claim with no citation is drift. Drop
     it, or propose it to the human as a ticket change.
  3. For each claim, record the questions that a careful reader would ask, each with your
     default answer (assumed). These questions stay dormant: asking them all now would bury
     the human in questions that may never matter. A question becomes to_ask when its claim
     is contested. For each option, add option(Q, 'Text', 'Effect'): what choosing it
     changes. Without options and effects, the human gets only an id, a text, and a default.
  4. Restate each claim as one or more concrete examples, for example "Submit on /checkout
     renders #6B3FA0". The human reads examples first. When the work has shifted up, the
     human, the agent and the CPU all use the formal language as the shared substrate, and the
     human reads, reviews and understands it, so write it to be read.
  5. Mark a claim visual when the human must see the result to judge it (orbit-kb help build).

Asking the human
  Build on your default assumptions, unless a wrong default is costly or impossible to undo
  (data loss, external effects, public interfaces, security, or money), or two defaults are
  equally likely and the build is large. Then ask before you build:
  - Ask at most three questions in one round. Choose them in this order. First, the questions
    whose default is the most costly if it is wrong. Then, the questions whose answers also
    settle other questions. The rest wait for the next round.
  - Give concrete options, and mark your default.
  - Do not ask the human to approve a list. A long list invites "approve all", and that is a
    quiet failure.
  In discovery, see also orbit-kb help discovery (ASKING).
  Who owns a question: the ledger owns a question that waits only on the hub owner, the
  tracker owns the others, and the spec KB pins both. When to pin: orbit-kb help build (PIN).

One writer
  Subagents of one pane share CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID, so they count as one writer. The
  writer lock does not keep them apart: two subagents that write one KB at once can
  interleave their records. Give a KB to one subagent at a time, or write it yourself.

Speed
  Run independent tool calls in parallel. Give long mechanical work (wide searches, long test
  runs) to a subagent on a fast model. A small task does not need a subagent.
  Report results, not process.

The records (0.8.0)
  orbit-kb show hypotheses       the goal first with its author, then each hypothesis under it with
                                 author, state, defeating case, premises and predictions
  orbit-kb adopt H --by human [--note 'the words']    the human adopts H (attest adoption)
  orbit-kb retire H --reason TEXT [--by human|agent]  retire H (attest retirement). An agent cannot
                                 retire a hypothesis that a human wrote or adopted, or one with adoption_cited.
  orbit-kb rests ID              "up:" what ID rests on, to the project goal; "down:" what rests on it
  Queries: goal_current(G)  hypothesis_state(H, S) (superseded, retired, defeated, adopted, proposed)
           hypothesis_current(H)  hypothesis_adopted(H)  tempered(H)  not_tempered(H)
           claim_of_goal(C)  hypothesis_guess(H)  rests_on(X, Y)  rests_up(X, Y)  goal_unsupported(G)
  current(C): no claim replaces C, and C is a claim of the goal or a current hypothesis predicts it.
  A query of hypothesis(H, K, T) answers as at 0.7.2 on every KB (it gives the goal), with a note.
